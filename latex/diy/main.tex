\begin{abstract}
Social bias in large language models is a well-documented harm, and many mitigation strategies have been proposed. These solutions share a structural limitation: each addresses bias on a particular dataset, a particular task, or a single bias dimension, and the gains rarely transfer beyond the slice they were designed for. In effect, existing methods teach a model to \emph{suppress} a specific symptom of bias rather than teach it \emph{how} to reduce bias as a behavior. LLMs are trained on human-produced text; humans are biased, and so models inherit that bias; but humans also have decades of social-psychology research on \emph{how} prejudice can be reduced, in the form of cognitive interventions validated in controlled studies. We propose to bring that literature into model training and inference: rather than narrow point-fixes, we teach models bias-reduction strategies, so that debiasing becomes a transferable bias intervention rather than a benchmark-specific patch. Concretely, we introduce \textsc{Debias It Yourself} (DIY), a framework that operationalizes five established prejudice-reducing interventions from social psychology, Stereotype Replacement, Counter-stereotypic Imaging, Individuation, Perspective-taking, and Positive Contact, instantiated through three complementary techniques: \emph{Show} (in-context demonstrations of intervention use), \emph{Teach} (instruction tuning on intervention-grounded supervision), and \emph{Revise} (intervention-guided self-revision). We evaluate DIY across a range of bias benchmarks against a broad set of debiasing baselines, and on reasoning benchmarks. DIY consistently lowers bias and outperforms existing baselines, with transferable cognitive behavior rather than dataset-specific suppression, generalizing across tasks, models, scales, and bias dimensions. Our code and data is available here{\footnote{\url{https://github.com/chahatraj/DebiasItYourself}}}.
\end{abstract}
