\begin{abstract}
Social bias in large language models is a well-documented harm, and many mitigations have been proposed---but each typically targets a single dataset, task, or bias dimension, teaching the model to \emph{suppress} a symptom rather than to \emph{reduce} bias as a behavior. We argue this is the wrong unit of supervision. LLMs are trained on human text and inherit human bias, yet decades of social-psychology research already show \emph{how} prejudice can be reduced in people through validated cognitive interventions. We bring that literature into model training and inference, teaching models the same bias-reduction strategies so that debiasing becomes a transferable skill rather than a benchmark-specific patch. We introduce \diy{} (DIY), which operationalizes five established prejudice-reducing interventions, \textsc{Stereotype Replacement}, \textsc{Counter-stereotypic Imaging}, \textsc{Individuation}, \textsc{Perspective-taking}, and \textsc{Positive Contact}, through three complementary techniques: \textbf{\textsc{Show}} (in-context demonstrations), \textbf{\textsc{Teach}} (instruction tuning), and \textbf{\textsc{Revise}} (intervention-guided self-revision). Evaluated against a broad set of debiasing baselines on bias and reasoning benchmarks, \diy{} consistently lowers bias and outperforms prior methods, with gains that generalize across tasks, models, scales, and bias dimensions---evidence that grounding mitigation in transferable cognitive behavior beats dataset-specific suppression. Code and data: \footnote{\url{https://github.com/chahatraj/DebiasItYourself}}.
\end{abstract}
