\begin{abstract}
Large language models routinely treat socially salient cues---names, group descriptors, roles, occupations, and institutional contexts---as task evidence, producing biased outputs even when these cues are irrelevant to the correct answer. We introduce \textsc{Debias It Yourself} (DIY), a structured supervision framework that translates five prejudice-reducing interventions from social psychology---Stereotype Replacement, Counter-stereotypic Imaging, Individuation, Perspective-taking, and Positive Contact---into a shared schema for both training and inference. Rather than proposing a single prompt template or psychology-labeled fine-tune, DIY defines a unified protocol for identifying unsupported social assumptions and producing task-consistent outputs, instantiated as a family of variants that compose three mechanisms: \emph{Show} (in-context demonstrations), \emph{Teach} (instruction tuning), and \emph{Revise} (intervention-guided second-pass inference). We evaluate ten configurations---Base, DIY-Teach, DIY-Show, DIY-Revise, DIY-Teach-Show, and DIY-Teach-Revise (each at 0/1-shot where applicable)---across CrowS-Pairs, StereoSet, BBQ, WinoBias, and WinoGender using a normalized bias error in which lower values consistently indicate better mitigation, and we additionally evaluate Balanced COPA and ARC to verify that bias reduction does not erode reasoning competence. On Llama-3.1-8B, Llama-3.1-70B, and Qwen, DIY reduces cross-benchmark bias error most consistently when intervention-guided revision is paired with structured supervision, and the pattern holds across model families and scales, suggesting that prejudice-reduction theory transfers into a portable inference-time and training-time recipe rather than a model-specific trick.
\end{abstract}
